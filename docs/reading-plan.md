# Where to start, saving your place, and the reading plan

## What the app remembers (on this device only)
- **Where you stopped:** updated when you read, listen, tap a word, open details or turn a page. "I read it" moves it to the next ayah.
- **Pinned places (📌):** up to 50, newest first.
- **Ayahs you marked "I read it":** self-reported. The app cannot know whether you really read.
- **Daily counts, streak and goal:** a streak is days in a row with at least one ayah read. If you have not read yet today the streak is not broken until the day ends. Days are the device's local calendar days.
- **The plan.**

Everything is saved in the browser (web) or the phone's storage (mobile). **Nothing is synced between devices or to a server yet**, so the web app and the phone app have separate progress, and clearing browser data deletes it. A server backup tied to an account is a possible next step.

## The reading plan
- Choose the length (30 / 60 / 90 days or 14-365 custom) and the order:
  - **Beginner path:** Al-Fatiha, then 112, 113, 114, then Juz Amma backwards from the shortest, then surahs 2 to 77.
  - **Mushaf order:** 1 to 114. At 30 days this is about 208 ayahs a day, which is one juz a day.
- The whole Quran (6,236 ayahs) is split into equal daily parts (they differ by at most one ayah). Tests prove there is no gap and no overlap for every length from 14 to 365 days.
- Each day also lists: **new words** (the 300 most frequent Quran words spread over the plan, 3-15 a day), a **flashcard review**, and an optional **Daily Arabic** task. Those last two are ticked by hand. The reading part is checked **automatically** from your "I read it" marks.
- **Status:** *on track* means every earlier day is finished (today may still be in progress, with half a day of slack); *behind* and *ahead* otherwise.
- **Re-plan:** the end date never moves. The unread ayahs from your first unread one are spread over the days that remain. Past reading is kept.

## What the plan does not do
- It does not estimate minutes: reading speed differs too much between people. It shows ayahs per day instead.
- It does not check that you understood anything. It tracks reading, not learning quality.
- Plans of 30 days mean about 200 ayahs a day. That is a reading pace; it is not realistic if you also study each word. Longer plans suit careful learners.
